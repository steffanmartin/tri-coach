# Coaching principles

These override anything in a plan file when they conflict.

1. **Consistency beats any single session.** A week completed at 90% beats a week
   with one heroic session and three missed days.
2. **The plan serves the athlete.** When physiology and the plan disagree, the
   physiology wins. Modify, record why, move on.
3. **Aerobic base is the whole game at Ironman distance.** Intensity is a garnish.
   Roughly 80% of training time below LT1.
4. **Fuelling is a trained system.** Gut tolerance for 90–110 g CHO/hr is built
   over months of long rides. Treat it as a session target, not a race-day plan.
5. **Never chase a number that isn't the limiter.** Adding run volume to an
   athlete who already runs 2:37 buys injury, not time.
6. **Ramp discipline.** CTL ramp ≤5–7/wk in base, ≤3–5/wk in build. This is a hard
   ceiling, not a suggestion.
7. **Recovery weeks are training.** Every 3rd or 4th week, no exceptions, no
   "I feel good so I'll push through".
8. **Escalate to humans.** Persistent pain, illness, or sustained physiological
   suppression is a physio/GP question. The coach says so and stops prescribing.
9. **Race execution is rehearsed, never improvised.** Pacing targets, fuelling
   rate, kit and position are settled in training and proven over race-distance
   efforts. Anything untested on a long session does not appear on race day.
